A metro-Denver moving-day checklist, week by week

Short, plain, and built around the things that go sideways in an in-town move: the truck has nowhere to park, the paperwork is missing, or the walkthrough is skipped.

Four weeks out

  • Collect three written quotes and confirm each company’s permit.
  • Book the date; ask about weekday versus weekend crews.
  • Tell the building manager (or HOA) you are moving, and ask about elevator reservations and insurance-certificate requirements.

Two weeks out

  • If you live inside the city and county of Denver, check whether the truck will need curb space. Denver’s Department of Transportation and Infrastructure takes street-occupancy permits online through E-Permits, and meter bagging through a separate online system. Per DOTI, bagging requests can take up to 72 hours to process and a week of notice is recommended. See DOTI Street Occupancy.
  • The DOTI page does not specifically mention moving trucks, so phone them to find out what your block needs. It also announces a permitting freeze from November 26, 2026 through January 3, 2027, so ask early if you move in that window.
  • Other metro cities set their own curb rules; call the city or ask your mover.

One week out

  • Pack a “day one” box: chargers, medications, toilet paper, tools, a box cutter.
  • Photograph valuables and the condition of furniture.
  • Reconfirm the arrival window and the crew size in writing.

The day before

  • Empty and defrost the fridge; disconnect washers.
  • Mark “do not load” items.
  • Clear a path from door to curb.

Moving day

  • Ask the crew lead for the paperwork before loading and compare the company name with your contract.
  • Walk each room with the lead at the end; empty closets and cupboards are where things get left.
  • Do not sign a “complete” slip until you have checked delivered items against your inventory. Note damage on the spot.

After the move

  • Keep every page of the contract and the receipts.
  • If something is damaged or missing, tell the mover in writing quickly, and use the PUC if the company will not respond.
